# Team and creative brief

## Confirmed context

Three people are building a Roblox game using a shared GitHub repository. The owner uses Claude Desktop, reports having Claude Max and ChatGPT Plus, and expects Claude to perform much of the implementation. Teammates have access to AI subscriptions too. Subscription availability is not evidence of Roblox development experience, asset-production capacity, or tool access.

ChatGPT/Codex will receive rough ideas, reference pictures, and feedback, then produce detailed prompts for Claude. The owner wants unusually ambitious presentation and very explicit instructions. Their standard is a result beyond what they could easily imagine themselves, while remaining a functioning Roblox game.

Claude should contribute ideas and ask useful questions. The owner explicitly accepts creative freedom to improve details within the plan. They do not want missing information disguised as certainty.

The team has not selected a game. The planned decision meeting is Sunday, October 18, 2026. Candidate ideas are recorded in file 05. None is a production commitment. Grow a Garden, stealing/collection games, and Bubble Gum Simulator-style hatching are reference interests, not instructions to copy their assets or systems wholesale. The exact experience meant by “Steal an Egg” and the exact Bubble Gum title/version remain unidentified.

The intended audience is mostly children. A temporary platform eligibility restriction does not change the intended creative audience into an adult audience. Design the experience appropriately and verify launch access separately.

## What exceptional should mean

1. A newcomer understands the central fantasy from the first playable view.
2. The core interaction feels rewarding before dozens of secondary systems exist.
3. Progress changes what players see, do, or can show friends; it is not only a larger number.
4. World design, UI, sound, lighting, animation, and effects feel like one game.
5. Players can identify an exciting next goal without navigating a wall of menus.
6. The game remains readable during its most spectacular moments.
7. Rewards, ownership, and saves remain correct under delay, retries, competition, and disconnects.
8. The team can maintain the result and add content without rewriting everything.

## Creative authority

**Proceed within approved scope:** improve spacing, hierarchy, transitions, feedback, naming of internal components, reusable organization, readability, and performance. Choose reversible details consistent with an approved visual direction. Record consequential assumptions.

**Recommend and get direction first:** choose the concept; merge concepts; add trading, PvP, breeding, paid luck, new currencies, destructive prestige, permanent theft, complex cross-server systems, or an entirely different art style. These change the promise, cost, or fairness of the game.

**Verify rather than guess:** APIs, saved-data compatibility, asset ownership, actual connector access, moderation outcomes, release eligibility, market rankings, and whether a test passed.

## Working defaults, not user commitments

Until device scope is decided, use phone and desktop layouts as a design-review baseline. Do not call console support complete without appropriate input and focus testing. Prefer free or existing project assets during exploration; no spending budget has been approved. Assume no launch deadline. Avoid assigning teammates roles based on their subscription plans.

## Open questions to raise at the point they matter

- Which concept and one-sentence player fantasy did the team select?
- Who owns each system, shared interface, map area, and release decision?
- Which platforms and representative devices are required for launch?
- What art references are mandatory, and which details are inspirational only?
- What are the asset budget, production skills, and realistic release window?
- Are collection losses temporary, recoverable, or permanent?
- Which monetization categories are acceptable to the team?
- What can the current Claude session actually read, change, and test?

Do not ask all of these before every task. Consult the current decision register and ask only what is still relevant and unresolved.
